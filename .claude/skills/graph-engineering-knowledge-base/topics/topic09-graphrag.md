# Topic 9: GraphRAG

## Core Idea
GraphRAG anchors LLM context in a structured knowledge graph instead of a flat vector database, dynamically constructing a bespoke **Context Graph** per query by traversing an RDF knowledge graph. This buys two capabilities standard vector RAG lacks — explainability (tracing the exact path of nodes/Holons used) and disambiguation (using GNN-learned structural embeddings to distinguish contextually different but linguistically/visually similar entities) — but makes retrieval quality entirely dependent on the underlying graph's structural integrity: "when the graph is wrong, RAG is wrong."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Context Graph**: a temporary, bespoke subgraph generated by traversing the RDF knowledge graph, tailored specifically to answer one query.
  - When to use: whenever an answer requires connecting facts across document/entity boundaries that a flat vector similarity search would keep disjointed.
  - How: the query triggers a graph traversal (not just a similarity lookup) that assembles the relevant nodes and their relationships into a subgraph, which becomes the LLM's grounding context.
- **Ontology-Guided Extraction for GraphRAG**: reuses the OWL/SHACL constraint-layer mechanism from Topics 3–4 to keep the knowledge graph feeding GraphRAG structurally sound, since GraphRAG's output quality is bounded by the graph's quality.
- **Agentic GraphRAG** *(term and mechanics not present in the source corpus — described here using outside knowledge per the corpus's own caveat; verify independently before treating as authoritative)*: marries knowledge graphs with dynamic multi-agent orchestration (Topics 11–12) — instead of one deterministic retrieval pass, an autonomous agent iteratively writes and executes graph queries (Cypher/SPARQL), evaluates the returned subgraph, decides whether to traverse further, and may fan out to query multiple graph domains in parallel before synthesizing an answer.
- **Knowledge Spine** *(term and mechanics not present in the source corpus — described here using outside knowledge per the corpus's own caveat; verify independently before treating as authoritative)*: a central, unified taxonomic backbone in enterprise data architecture. Rather than siloed per-department knowledge graphs, domain-specific graphs (HR, Sales, Engineering) branch off a shared top-level ontology, so an entity like "Customer ID" resolves to the same underlying entity across every domain graph.

## Key Concepts
- **Explainability**: users can trace the specific path of holons and nodes the LLM traversed to formulate its answer — a capability vector RAG's flat chunk retrieval does not provide.
- **Disambiguation**: leveraging structural context from GNN-learned embeddings, the LLM distinguishes entities that are linguistically or visually similar but contextually different (directly reusing the GNN disambiguation mechanism from Topic 10's PinSage discussion).
- **"When the Graph Is Wrong, RAG Is Wrong"**: GraphRAG's output is entirely bounded by the structural integrity of its underlying graph — the same LLM-assisted KGE failure modes from Topic 4, especially Entity Flattening, propagate directly into GraphRAG hallucination or failure.

## Mental Models
- **GraphRAG doesn't fix hallucination, it relocates the point of failure**: instead of the LLM hallucinating an answer from scratch, GraphRAG makes the LLM's grounding only as good as the graph it traverses — which means graph quality assurance (Topics 3–4's ontology-guided extraction and SHACL validation) is not a separate concern from GraphRAG reliability, it *is* GraphRAG reliability.
- **Multi-hop reasoning across document boundaries is the specific gap this fills**: vector RAG's fundamental limitation isn't retrieval speed, it's that similarity search over chunks has no native way to connect disparate facts scattered across many different documents into one coherent multi-hop answer — a Context Graph traversal does this by construction. *(Note: the corpus flags that it does not detail the specific enterprise-scale motivations for migrating from vector RAG to graph-based RAG; the multi-hop-reasoning framing here is drawn from the corpus's own outside-knowledge caveat, not from its sourced material.)*
- **Explainability and disambiguation are the concrete, sourced payoffs — treat everything beyond them as less certain**: the corpus is explicit and well-sourced on these two capabilities; the Agentic GraphRAG and Knowledge Spine concepts, while plausible and consistent with the rest of the corpus, are flagged by the corpus itself as filled in from outside knowledge and warrant independent verification before being treated as settled fact.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating vector RAG and GraphRAG as interchangeable**: applying flat vector similarity search to a question that fundamentally requires multi-hop, cross-document reasoning will underperform regardless of embedding quality — the retrieval mechanism, not just the embedding model, is the limiting factor.
- **Skipping graph quality assurance because "GraphRAG is just retrieval"**: since GraphRAG output quality is bounded by graph structural integrity, deploying GraphRAG on a graph built without ontology-guided extraction and SHACL validation (Topic 4) inherits every one of those unmitigated failure modes, notably Entity Flattening.

## Worked Example
A legal-tech company builds a system to answer "which of our client's subsidiaries have contracts referencing a force majeure clause similar to the one in the Q3 dispute?" A vector-RAG approach embeds and searches contract text chunks by similarity — it can surface individually similar clauses but has no native mechanism to connect "this subsidiary" to "this contract" to "this specific clause instance" across potentially hundreds of separate documents. A GraphRAG approach instead has an underlying knowledge graph where `Subsidiary`, `Contract`, and `Clause` are typed nodes connected by relationships (`OWNS`, `CONTAINS_CLAUSE`, `SIMILAR_TO`); the query triggers a Context Graph traversal starting from the Q3 dispute clause, following `SIMILAR_TO` edges to related clauses, then `CONTAINS_CLAUSE`/`OWNS` edges back to subsidiaries — assembling a subgraph that directly answers the multi-hop question, with every hop traceable for explainability. If the underlying knowledge graph has an Entity Flattening error (a subsidiary recorded as a string instead of a linked node — Topic 4), that subsidiary silently drops out of the Context Graph traversal and the answer is incomplete without any error being raised — the concrete mechanism behind "when the graph is wrong, RAG is wrong."

## Key Takeaways
1. GraphRAG's core mechanism is the Context Graph: a per-query, traversal-assembled subgraph, not a static retrieval index.
2. The two sourced, concrete advantages over vector RAG are explainability (traceable node/Holon paths) and disambiguation (GNN-structural context) — lead with these when justifying GraphRAG adoption.
3. GraphRAG reliability is inseparable from knowledge graph quality assurance — Entity Flattening and other KGE failures (Topic 4) propagate directly into GraphRAG failures.
4. Agentic GraphRAG and the Knowledge Spine concept are plausible extensions consistent with the rest of the corpus but are explicitly flagged as filled in from outside knowledge, not sourced — treat with appropriate caution.
5. The motivating gap GraphRAG fills is multi-hop, cross-document reasoning that flat vector similarity search structurally cannot provide.
