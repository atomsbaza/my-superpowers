# Appendix A: Resources

## Core Idea
Pragmatic Programmers should always be learning; this appendix is a curated (and by 1999-2000 standards, now largely dated) pointer to professional societies, periodicals, books, web sites, and internet tools the authors themselves used, plus the book's full bibliography.

## Key Concepts
- **Professional societies**: the authors recommend joining the ACM (Association for Computing Machinery) and/or the IEEE Computer Society (or a national equivalent, e.g., the BCS in the UK) for conferences, special interest groups, standards participation, and publications.
- **Building a library**: a curated list of periodicals (IEEE Computer, IEEE Software, Communications of the ACM, SIGPLAN, Dr. Dobbs Journal, The Perl Journal, Software Development Magazine) and books grouped by topic — Analysis and Design (Meyer's Object-Oriented Software Construction, the Gang of Four's Design Patterns, Fowler's Analysis Patterns), Teams and Projects (Brooks' The Mythical Man Month, McCarthy's Dynamics of Software Development, Cockburn's Surviving Object-Oriented Projects), and Specific Environments (Unix, Windows, C++).
- **Internet resources**: a list of URLs (numbered [URL 1] through [URL 58]) for editors (Emacs, vi), compilers/languages/tools (GCC, Java, Perl, Python, Eiffel variants, Sather, Squeak, TOM), Unix-under-DOS tools, source code control systems (RCS, CVS, ClearCase, PVCS, Visual SourceSafe, Perforce), and papers/publications (comp.object FAQ, Extreme Programming, Cockburn's, Fowler's, and Martin's home pages, the Demeter Project).
- **Bibliography**: the full citation list ([Bak72] through [You95]) backing every reference made throughout the book — includes foundational works like Brooks' Mythical Man Month [Bro95], the Gang of Four's Design Patterns [GHJV95], Fowler et al.'s Refactoring [FBB+99], Lieberherr and Holland's original Law of Demeter paper [LH89], and Knuth's Art of Computer Programming volumes [Knu97a/b, Knu98].

## Mental Models
- Treat this appendix as a snapshot of "what a well-read pragmatic programmer of 1999 kept on their shelf and their bookmarks bar" — useful as a historical anchor for the book's other chapters' citations, not as a live resource list (many URLs and some periodicals are now defunct or superseded).
- Use the book-topic-to-book mapping (Analysis and Design / Teams and Projects / Specific Environments) as a template for building your own curated reading list per professional area.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating a link list as permanent**: the authors themselves flag that "the nature of the Web means that many of these links may well be stale by the time you read this book" — a caution equally true (more so) decades later.

## Key Takeaways
1. Join a professional society (ACM and/or IEEE Computer Society) for standards participation, conferences, and publications.
2. Curate a personal reading list across analysis/design, teams/projects, and your specific technical environment — don't rely solely on this now-dated appendix.
3. Treat any link list (including this one) as perishable; expect to re-find current equivalents via search rather than trusting old URLs.
4. The bibliography is a useful map back to primary sources for concepts referenced elsewhere in the book (Law of Demeter, Refactoring, Design Patterns, The Mythical Man Month).
