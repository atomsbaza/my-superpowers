# Glossary

**Architecture characteristic** — A quality important to system success that influences structure and can be specified or measured (Ch 4).

**Architecture decision** — A significant rule or choice that constrains system construction (Ch 1, 19).

**Architecture fitness function** — An automated objective assessment that protects an architecture characteristic during change (Ch 6).

**Architecture quantum** — An independently deployable artifact with high functional cohesion and synchronous connascence (Ch 7).

**Bounded Context** — A boundary within which a domain model and language remain consistent (Ch 7, 17).

**Cohesion** — The degree to which responsibilities within a module belong together (Ch 3).

**Connascence** — A relationship where changing one element requires changing or coordinating another (Ch 3).

**Coupling** — Dependency between modules or architectural elements (Ch 3).

**Design principle** — Guidance for choosing among options rather than an absolute construction rule (Ch 1).

**Elasticity** — Ability to add and remove capacity as load changes (Ch 4).

**Event-driven architecture** — A style that communicates through events and asynchronous message flow (Ch 14).

**Fitness function** — See Architecture fitness function.

**Functional cohesion** — Responsibilities in a unit contribute to a coherent capability (Ch 7).

**Granularity** — The size and responsibility scope of an architectural unit (Ch 7, 13, 17).

**Layered architecture** — Technical layers, commonly presentation, business, and data, with controlled dependencies (Ch 10).

**Microkernel architecture** — Stable core system extended by plug-in components (Ch 12).

**Microservices architecture** — Independently deployable services with strong data and operational ownership (Ch 17).

**Modular monolith** — One deployment unit with explicit internal domain boundaries (Ch 18).

**Pipeline architecture** — Data flows through connected transformation filters (Ch 11).

**Risk storming** — Collaborative identification, consensus, and mitigation of architecture risk (Ch 20).

**Saga** — Distributed business transaction composed of local transactions and compensating actions (Ch 17).

**Service-based architecture** — Coarse-grained services providing business capabilities, often with pragmatic data sharing (Ch 13).

**Space-based architecture** — Scalable processing units and virtualized in-memory middleware used to avoid centralized bottlenecks (Ch 15).

**Technical breadth** — Awareness of many technologies and solution families (Ch 2, 24).

**Technical partitioning** — Component organization by technical concern rather than business capability (Ch 8).

**Trade-off** — A choice that improves one quality or constraint while making another harder; the First Law says every architectural choice has one (Ch 1).
